# Rethink, Nov 28: start from the site that worked

The Principal: "a pretty decent website was built a month and a half ago without all this bloated
overload so just start there." This replaces `docs/review-2026-11-28-stop.md`, which offered known
options instead of rethinking.

## 1. The site that worked (Oct 12)

Screens: `runtime-content/miopages-finalization/home-1440.png` (and the other pages beside it).
- **Seven pages,** each doing one job. The menu had five items plus "Get Your Free Review": Why
  MioPages, What You Get, How It Works, Questions & Answers.
- **Short.** Home was about 600 words and about 3,600 pixels tall; most pages were 300–600 words.
- **Four kinds of section, each drawn well:** an opening, a short statement, a set of three to six
  short points, and a call to action. Sections sat in calm alternating bands (cream, lavender,
  indigo), with the real logo and a warm accent button.
- **No pictures, no FPO frames, no "Content needed" boxes, no bordered panels.** It looked finished
  anyway.
- **Words** drafted, then edited by people (the R3 editorial pass).
- **Rendered by this app's own pages** (`components/managed-sites/focus-page.tsx`,
  `app/focus-presentation.css`), from a plain content file. Both are still in the code, unused.

## 2. What we make now (Nov 28)

- **Ten pages,** with a nine-item menu that doesn't fit one line, so even a wide desktop screen
  shows the phone menu.
- **Home is about 1,500 words and about 9,400 pixels tall;** other pages are 1,000–1,600 words.
- **Any structure Core composes,** node by node: panels, columns and nested groups.
- **17 FPO frames, many "Content needed" boxes,** and pictures in most sections.
- **The same story told on every page,** each with its own call to action.

## 3. Why it grew: nothing ever subtracts

Core's own page instructions (`lib/platform/intake/core-authoring-instructions.ts`) say:
- "You receive everything Core knows … Use all of it."
- "Write and design the one requested Page completely."
- "Every section must give the reader a different reason …"
- "Make the site visually rich. Plan an image wherever it helps … Otherwise still place the image
  node." That makes FPO frames everywhere.
- "Never leave the spot out just because the material is missing." That makes a "Content needed"
  box everywhere.

Each of these was a fair correction to an earlier thin or empty site (P0-9, P0-12, P0-19, #12),
then written as an absolute. Nothing in the instructions asks for brevity, sets a length, or gives
anyone the job of cutting. Every page is also written alone, so each one re-explains the whole
service. Everything around it adds too: the free-form layout, the rules check, the mending.

The October site had a counterweight these lack: a small fixed design that only does a few things
well, and people whose job was to cut.

The project itself shows the same pattern. It now has 91 documents (22,774 lines), 24,500 lines
of library code and 142 test files, and the site is worse than the 7-page one.

## 4. The idea: make Core work the way the October site was made

Put Core in the roles people played in October, and keep the counterweight.

1. **Plan from the visitor's questions, not from everything Core knows.** A visitor to a small
   service business asks five or six things: what is this, is it for me, what do I get, how does it
   work, can I trust it, how do I start. The Site plan answers those, one page each, plus what the
   business truly needs beyond them. That makes the five-item menu of October, not ten pages.
2. **Write the whole site as one short script first,** all pages together, at the October length.
   Then Core edits it as an editor would: cut repetition across pages, one idea per section, one
   call to action where it belongs. Only then split it into pages. This is the R3 role.
3. **Build pages from the few section kinds the October renderer drew well:** opening, statement,
   set of points, call to action. Add a new kind only when a real business needs one: questions,
   steps, a comparison. Core chooses the kinds and the words. The renderer, not Core, owns the
   layout. Each Client's look comes from what a designer would choose for them: logo, color, type,
   picture style, the rhythm of the bands. That's how October looked like MioPages.
4. **Pictures only where a real one exists. Missing material stays off the page.** A testimonial or
   price that doesn't exist yet is listed for the Client and the Specialist in the workspace and
   portal, not drawn as a box on the page; the page doesn't make the claim. *This changes how Part
   1 #12 is applied (FPO and visible placeholders), so it's the Principal's call.* The October site
   shows a page reads as finished without them.
5. **A person still edits.** A Specialist's hand edit in WordPress is already supported and kept
   (Part 1 #7: human work is a normal part of the service). The October words had that.
6. **WordPress gets the October look.** The four section kinds become the managed theme's own block
   patterns, styled as `focus-presentation.css` styled them, so drafts on miopages.org look like
   October and stay editable.

**What it removes:** free-form layout trees, "visually rich" FPO frames, placeholder boxes on
pages, ten-page plans, page-by-page writing, and the machinery that policed them.

**What it keeps:** the record of every change, the Report, research and strategy, delivery to
WordPress, hand edits, the Client's authority over facts, and words kept separate from layout.

## 5. How to see whether it works, before building much

1. **Free:** render the October site from its own content file on this PC (the renderer is still in
   the code) as the target, and put today's C0 pages beside it.
2. **About $1, Principal's OK first:** Core writes C0's site script once, under the new
   instructions (visitor questions, October length, the four section kinds, nothing about FPO or
   placeholders). Render it with the October renderer and compare with October by eye.
3. **Only if it holds up:** port the four section kinds to the WordPress theme, and change the Site
   plan and page steps to match.
