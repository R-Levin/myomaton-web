## Home

**H1** A website that keeps up with your business

**Search description** Launch a first site or improve the one you have. MioPages helps small businesses explain their work, earn trust and give visitors a clear next step.

### Give your website a job

When someone hears about your business, your website is often where they go to decide whether to call, book or ask a question. It should answer what you do, who you can help and what happens next. MioPages is building a managed service around that job, from planning and launch through the changes that come later.

[Explore the managed service](#service) · [See what the Free Review covers](#free-web-presence-review)

### Start where you are

**You already have a website.** Some of what you have may be worth keeping. Start with the pages, links and tools people rely on, then decide which parts no longer serve the business. [Review your existing site options](#already-have-a-site)

**You’re getting started.** Give prospects one trustworthy place to understand your offer and take the next step. Start with the business you’re ready to serve, even if you don’t have an address for your website yet. [Plan a first website](#starting-fresh)

### Keep it useful after launch

Your services, examples and contact details will change. The site needs someone to notice, decide what matters and make approved changes. Say you start getting questions about work you don’t take on. Updating one service page may be more useful than adding a new article. We’ll agree what evidence would tell us whether the change helped. [See how Ongoing works](#ongoing-management)

### Choose a next step

The Free Review is for owners who want a useful second look before deciding what to do. If you already know what needs attention, tell us what you’re trying to achieve and what is in the way. [Free Review](#free-web-presence-review) · [Discuss a project](#talk-about-your-project)

## Service

**H1** One managed service, from the first plan to ongoing improvement

**Search description** See what a MioPages Launch covers, what Ongoing takes care of and which decisions stay with your business.

### Work out what the site needs to do

We start with what you offer, who it serves and what a good inquiry looks like. For a business with an existing presence, we look at which pages and tools people depend on. If the Free Review already answered something, that information comes forward. You correct our understanding before it becomes the basis for your website.

### Build around the decisions visitors need to make

Launch turns the business understanding into pages, navigation and a clear visitor action. We propose how to talk about your services, what proof fits, and which information people need before getting in touch. You review that direction and the site. Before an agreed launch, check the contact path, identify any continuity work, and decide what first-party measurement is appropriate.

### Know when the work needs a separate scope

Standard Launch is intended for ordinary supported business-site work. A larger migration or an unusual dependency can call for an expanded scope agreed before work starts. If you need a custom application or advanced commerce, bring it up early. We can explain which parts fit the managed service and which would need another arrangement. [Plan an existing-site handoff](#already-have-a-site)

### Share the right responsibilities

You know the business. You confirm facts, authorize access when needed, provide material we can use and approve important changes. MioPages handles supported site work and explains what it recommends. Before committing, ask which tools and accounts you control, which assets can transfer and what happens if the arrangement ends. We’ll make those terms part of the scope discussion.

### Plan for the work that follows

Launch creates a useful starting point for Ongoing. Routine updates, operation and improvements build on that work as the business changes. We’ll agree priorities and confirm supported scope when we talk. [Explore Ongoing management](#ongoing-management) · [Discuss Launch scope](#talk-about-your-project)

## Already have a site?

**H1** Improve the site without losing what people already use

**Search description** Plan what to keep, improve or replace on an existing small-business website, including useful pages, links, forms and account access.

### Start with the places people depend on

Send the site address and tell us which visitor action matters most. It might be a request for a quote, a call about a specific service or a handoff into a booking system. We’ll look for the parts that help visitors act and the parts that make the job harder. Tell us about any pages, links or tools your staff or customers share often.

### Decide what stays, what changes and what is retired

An accurate service explanation may be worth keeping. An out-of-date homepage may need a different approach. A page for a service you no longer sell may need retiring, while links that people still use require their own plan. These are separate decisions. We’ll look at content and dependencies before recommending changes.

### Bring the tricky parts up early

An existing form, email setup, account portal or specialized integration may affect what a safe handoff involves. A first conversation can identify the dependencies. Review access and migration details after looking at the site. Keep passwords and access details out of the inquiry. We’ll agree separately on who needs access and how it should be provided.

### Choose how to start

If you’re unsure whether your website needs a full replacement, start with the Free Review. If there is a specific timing or access issue, describe it in a conversation before committing. [Request a review of your current presence](#free-web-presence-review) · [Discuss a handoff](#talk-about-your-project)

## Starting fresh?

**H1** Give your first customers a clear place to go

**Search description** Plan a credible first website around your offer, the people it serves and the first useful step they can take. A current website is optional.

### Lead with what people can buy

Tell us what you’re ready to offer, whom it helps and what a visitor needs to know before contacting you. A clear service description and a useful way to inquire can do more than a long menu. If you’re still deciding exactly whom you can serve or how work begins, we’ll identify the decisions that affect the site first.

### Build trust with evidence you already have

Use verifiable qualifications, your real operating experience, and examples you have permission to show. Explain how an engagement works when you don’t yet have reviews or results to share. The site can earn trust through specifics: what information to send, where you work, what you can handle and what happens after an inquiry.

### Leave room to learn after Launch

You may discover that prospects ask a different first question than you expected. The site should be able to change as you learn. Start with the pages that answer meaningful decisions, then add or revise content when a real need appears. [See what happens after Launch](#ongoing-management)

### Start with a short description of the business

Describe the offer and the next step you want visitors to take. You can start a scope discussion with no existing website. If you already have a public profile or a temporary landing page and want a second look, a Free Review may be useful. [Talk about your first website](#talk-about-your-project) · [Understand the Free Review](#free-web-presence-review)

## Ongoing management

**H1** Keep the site useful as your business changes

**Search description** Understand how MioPages plans site updates, reviews inquiry evidence and prioritizes content or conversion improvements after launch.

### Keep important information accurate

When a service changes, customers need the current information whether they arrive through a referral, search or an old bookmark. We identify the pages affected, check the business facts with you, and update the parts that need to change within the agreed service scope. If something urgent breaks the visitor path, the first job is to get that path working again.

### Use evidence to decide what to change

Here is an illustrative example. Suppose people visit a service page and submit inquiries, but a number ask for a service you don’t offer. We would confirm the boundary with you, propose clearer service copy, and check later inquiries for the same confusion after the wording changes. If there is too little evidence to tell, the report should say so. A form submission alone cannot tell us whether it turned into useful work.

### See what happened and what’s next

A useful update explains what changed, why, what we can observe since then and what needs a decision. An update might recommend adding an answer to a recurring buying question. Another might put a contact-path repair before any new content. You should be able to see how the work connects to the business and comment on the next priority.

## Free Web Presence Review

**H1** Find out what your Web Presence needs next

**Search description** Ask for an initial look at your public presence, the issues that matter most and a useful next step, with no requirement to commit to Launch.

### What we’ll look at

Tell us what your business does, which public website or profile you want us to look at, and what you want visitors to do. We’ll look at what the presence communicates, where a visitor might get stuck and what could help. If there isn’t enough public material for a useful review, a short conversation may make more sense.

### What a useful Review gives you

You should be able to see what we understood, which observations matter, what we would address first, and a useful next step. Here is an illustration: a company offers three services but its home page gives them equal weight, while most referrals come for one. We might recommend putting that service first and making sure the contact path works for its visitors. The real recommendation would depend on evidence from the business. An illustrative Home concept may accompany the Review when it adds useful clarity.

### Choose whether to go further

Use the Review to decide your next step. You can ask about one recommendation, continue into a Launch scope discussion or keep working with your current arrangement. If you continue, the findings and answers already supplied should carry forward. Paid onboarding goes deeper before producing the business report and a site proposal.

## Editorial review against service-led v3

The earlier accepted four-page design presented one clear service, a distinct Review, a conceptual Hero and a focused Experience path. This version keeps that clarity, separates two materially different buyer decisions, makes Ongoing concrete and brings the intake paths together with explicit validation requirements. It does less with named proof because the relevant evidence and permissions are unresolved. If historical examples become usable, compare their value with the accepted Experience page before adding another route.

The revision sharpened repeated “we handle it” passages into distinct decisions: scope on Service, continuity on Existing Website, first trust signals on New Website and an evidence-led change on Ongoing. It kept project claims, pricing, rankings, guaranteed results and turnaround out of the public copy. Competitor pages informed buyer questions and route choices. The wording and examples were drafted for MioPages; no external copy, imagery or distinctive layout was reused. This was a human/Codex editorial comparison of the sample, not an automated originality audit or browser comparison of the new design.

For review, trace three paths: an established owner who is worried about changing an account-dependent site; a founder who has no site address; and a legacy customer who only wants their hosting account. Then check whether the Ongoing example makes the service worth considering. Return factual corrections separately from copy or navigation choices. Functional destinations, preview permissions, customer approval and eventual publication each have their own gates.
