# Fictional Customer 2: Tree-service onboarding scenario

**Status: owner-supplied fictional test case, November 9, 2026. Not a real customer, not approved customer facts, not evidence of implemented functionality.** Use alongside [the full paid onboarding knowledge-transfer contract](paid-onboarding-knowledge-transfer-contract.md) and [C0 adaptive walkthrough](c0-adaptive-onboarding-walkthrough.md). This example is intended to test whether the platform genuinely understands an ordinary prospect's existing information and intelligently asks what is missing, rather than supplying an identical ten-screen questionnaire. It does **not** redefine the C0 founder-led special collaboration or require such intensive strategic consulting for normal customers.

## 1. What the fictional customer's existing website tells us

The existing, **outdated website** describes an **established tree-service company** operating in **Bucks and Montgomery Counties, Pennsylvania**, primarily serving **homeowners and small businesses**. Its **primary services** are tree maintenance (including preventative maintenance) and **emergency tree service**. A **secondary service** is yard clearing and similar work; **tertiary/seasonal work** includes firewood and winter plowing. Customers can make contact by **phone, text or form/email**. The business has **no formal CRM**. **Emergency contacts usually arrive by phone**. The business accepts **cash, credit, Zelle, PayPal and Venmo**. It offers **free estimates**, reports **more than ten years' service history**, and says proof of **bonding and insurance** is available. **Testimonials are likely available** but have not been supplied or verified. The website **has no blog or content-marketing function**. The owner's assessment is that the current site **should likely be replaced**, rather than treated as an appropriate base to keep unchanged.

**Source/provenance correction (owner, November 9):** All of the business details above were obtained from the fictional company's **existing, outdated website**, not volunteered in an onboarding interview or directly confirmed by the company. Treat them as **unverified existing-site observations/claims requiring owner confirmation**, not as trusted customer-supplied or confirmed business truth. No specific site URL, real business name, actual phone number, addresses, hours, service terms or licenses has been provided for an independent inspection. This is an explicitly fictional scenario, not evidence of a website actually inspected by ChatGPT or Codex. Existing copy and payment logos also require rights/currentness checks before reuse.

## 2. Immediate system understanding — do not ask these generic questions again

The system should **begin with a provisional site-derived assessment**, summarize the extracted information naturally, and invite corrections, instead of asking the owner to repeat site content as though nothing is known. Group findings as structured **unconfirmed** candidates:

- **Business identity/geography:** tree service, established, Bucks and Montgomery Counties, PA. Exact service municipalities, public address and business name unknown.
- **Audience:** residential homeowners and small-business owners. Their different needs and decision journeys remain to be explored.
- **Offer priorities:** routine/preventive tree maintenance and emergency tree work = primary; yard clearing = secondary; firewood and snowplowing = tertiary/seasonal. Avoid portraying every service as an equal priority or implying the business is chiefly a firewood vendor.
- **Conversions:** emergency visitors most likely need immediate phone contact. Routine estimates may be appropriate for phone/text/form; the preferred main path and actual recipient/contact details require confirmation. No CRM currently; don't require or invent one.
- **Payment information:** cash, credit, Zelle, PayPal and Venmo reportedly accepted; payment handling is not automatically an ecommerce/online-checkout requirement.
- **Trust and fit:** reported 10+ years' history and free estimates. Bonding/insurance evidence may be obtainable and needs verification/current documentation before publication. Testimonials are tentative until examples and permissions are supplied.

A good opening is: “I reviewed the information on your current website. Here's what it appears to say about your tree-service business, your services and how people contact you. I also understand the site is outdated and may need to be replaced. Please correct anything no longer accurate; then we'll fill the important gaps so the new site can explain what you actually do.” The system must not claim to have inspected a real URL when none was supplied.

## 3. Real follow-up conversations — relevant depth without a fixed question cap

**First offering: ongoing tree care.** “When you say tree maintenance, which jobs do you routinely perform—trimming/pruning, inspections or hazard evaluations, disease management, removal, or something else? Which of these do you **not** offer?” (The examples are questions, **not inferred services**.) For selected services, optionally follow with typical customer situation, seasonality, included/excluded work, whether permits or site visits are necessary, whether estimates are free and how quickly a normal lead can expect a response. If the company provides materials, extract candidate descriptions and return for correction rather than repeatedly asking.

**Emergency tree service, a distinct high-priority path.** “What situations qualify as an emergency you can handle? Do you answer emergency calls outside regular business hours? What geographic coverage, actual response expectations and service limitations can you confirm?” Do **not** assume 24/7 availability, guaranteed arrival time, emergency storm removals, crane work, utility-line work or service in every municipality. Distinguish emergency **call now** from standard **request a free estimate**, and verify the published phone number and after-hours routing before site launch.

**Customers and trust.** “What tree problems prompt homeowners to contact you before they become emergencies? What does a small business need to know that a homeowner doesn't? What do people typically ask or worry about before hiring you?” These answers inform early-stage educational content, qualifiers, audience-specific explanations, trust language and conversion. Request available insurance/bonding documentation, accurate business history and appropriate permission-cleared testimonials or photos. Don't automatically treat “likely testimonials” as known proof.

**Secondary and seasonal services.** Yard clearing: which work, where, what is excluded and when to request a quote? Firewood: pickup vs delivery, area, product options, minimums, seasonal availability and contact route only if relevant. Snowplowing: residential vs small-commercial, relevant service areas, scheduling/pricing arrangement and seasonal availability. Don't over-expand into full equally weighted website sections without a reason.

**Current online presence and content.** An existing outdated site is **known to exist**; request its URL and inspect it with permission rather than asking whether the company has a site. Start with the customer's *likely replace* preference while checking what must remain continuous. Inventory pages, copy, media, service descriptions, contact forms, public numbers, old URLs, potential search traffic and any useful rankings/backlinks using actual evidence. Classify each as **retain / improve / replace / retire / explicitly exclude / investigate**, including what the owner **does not want** carried over. The old site has **no blog/content-marketing function**: do not invent a blog history or describe migration of nonexistent articles. Instead explore an appropriate initial content/FAQ/article strategy as a new managed capability, drawing on common tree-care questions, seasonality, prevention and emergency guidance, with claims reviewed by the owner. Ask how customers currently find/contact the company, whether phone and SMS numbers differ, and whether form/email submissions reach a dependable recipient.

**Contact and operations.** Confirm the real public name, phone and text destinations, email/form recipient, service hours and after-hours emergency answering procedure. Ask whether the business wants a single website contact form or separate routine service/estimate questions; do not require a CRM. Collect appropriate customer business contact information in **scoped private records** and make publishable contact details explicit. No customer payment credentials, passwords or private client information in an interview field.

The system invites longer natural descriptions, relevant documents/photos and multiple proof/service records if useful; it does not limit the total number of material questions. Organize the next few based on answered gaps, urgency and the build impact. Details that don't affect the initial site can remain optional without suppressing new information the customer wants to provide.

## 4. A provisional assessment and first-site direction, **not yet a final plan**

A grounded initial assessment might say:

“The business has a clear local service area, two valuable primary use cases—planned tree care and urgent tree problems—and distinct supporting/seasonal services. An effective first site should make **emergency phone contact prominent**, give planned-maintenance visitors a straightforward way to request a free estimate, and demonstrate credibility through accurate experience and confirmed insurance/bonding or testimonials. Service-specific information is presently too thin to draft trustworthy detail pages. Emergency hours and coverage, actual job types/exclusions, public contact routing and rights-backed proof require confirmation. The existing site is reportedly outdated and lacks a blog/content-marketing function, making a **replacement Managed Site with a new content foundation** a plausible recommendation. Its specific design, performance, SEO, URLs and technical condition remain unassessed because no URL or independent audit was supplied.”

**Possible initial architecture (recommendation only):** Home, Tree Care, Emergency Service, Other/Seasonal Services, About/Experience, Contact/Free Estimate, plus an appropriate **new** helpful-articles/FAQ content capability; perhaps location guidance and useful seasonal education. Do not invent a universal page count or require equal pages for yard clearing, firewood and plowing. SEO/location pages should depend on real service-area evidence rather than fabricated town coverage. Confirm emergency and routine conversion paths, not merely CTA labels.

**Current assessment status:** *useful provisional website-derived understanding, not confirmed and not build-ready*. Next owner questions should resolve whether the website's claimed services and priorities are still accurate, specific tree-care job types and exclusions, emergency contact/availability, public business identity and contact details, trust/evidence, seasonal services, any legacy pages/URLs worth retaining or excluding, and which genuinely helpful content topics should be introduced. When answers are supported, present a revised understanding and proposed site direction for factual confirmation, not an auto-published site.

## 5. Acceptance benchmark for Codex and the normal onboarding design

1. The system accepts the scenario as **existing-site-derived information**, preserving observations as structured, attributable **unconfirmed** knowledge candidates, and **does not ask the customer to re-enter** them in generic ten-step fields. Owner confirmation, rather than site text alone, establishes publishable truth.
2. It distinguishes primary/emergency, secondary and tertiary/seasonal offerings and adjusts the follow-up depth and recommended site priorities.
3. It proposes **emergency call vs ordinary estimate/contact** paths based on stated intent; it **does not** assume a 24/7 guarantee or real contact details.
4. It requests exact necessary job types/exclusions, operating details, public destinations and proof permissions rather than treating “tree care” as sufficient copy.
5. It allows long natural descriptions, repeatable service/proof records and future customer-supplied existing-site materials without a fixed total question limit.
6. It avoids forcing a CRM or e-commerce workflow because the business takes digital payments.
7. Its first assessment clearly distinguishes **old website observations**, **customer-confirmed facts**, unresolved details, feasible recommendations and unverified claims; it says what is and is not sufficient for the site.
8. C0's founder-specific strategy collaboration remains distinct from the ordinary Customer 2 experience, which should be thorough, low-friction, prepared and mostly managed by the platform.
9. An actual website/asset inspection and upload capability must be implemented, authorized and tested before claiming it works; sample responses are *design expectations*, not working code.
10. Recognizes the **outdated existing site** as the information source and likely **replacement** candidate, while separately auditing useful content, destination URLs and contact continuity before retirements/redirects.
11. Recognizes the **absence of a blog/content-marketing function** as a content opportunity and proposes suitable new educational content without inventing prior articles, without promising a fixed content quota and without granting permission to publish unverified tree-care claims.

**No production records, live local sessions, legacy services, DNS or WordPress generation are authorized by this scenario.**
